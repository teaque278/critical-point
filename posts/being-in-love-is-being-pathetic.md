You cannot be in love if you’re not pathetic. Now, being pathetic can have different meanings across different contexts and perspectives. I’m talking specifically about people in a relationship, or in love *(the two need not necessarily be synonymous)*, and the perspective is that of an outsider looking in. This outsider doesn’t even have to be a third person, it can simply be the same person taking a step back and observing their own behaviour as a result of being in love. They shall see then, it is quite pathetic.

The idea in itself goes completely against the notion—that I firmly believe in—that one should never let go of their dignity for the sake of love. Let’s begin right there. What does it mean to let go of your dignity in a relationship? It’s often attributed to losing composure, acting on negative impulses or taking below the belt shots at your partner out of spite. The fact that these actions come up during emotionally fragile or heated moments is proof that the thought of it was always there but something kept it veiled. This veil is your dignity and the act of letting go of it, is pathetic. No doubt. It is pathetic and unbecoming of a partner to say things that cannot be taken back even if it was in a moment of weakness. But the absolute truth is of little significance when you’re in love. You understand that an unkind word from them does not undo the things you love them for. You understand the subtext of hurt within their absurd and insensitive words. And sometimes, that understanding is enough to forgive them. What does it make of you when you let go off a partner’s unkind act because you love them?

There is a negative connotation to the word *‘pathetic’*. In fact, it is less a connotation and more just the meaning of it. The textbook definition of pathetic is *“causing a feeling of sadness, pity or lack of respect as a result of someone’s weakness or inadequacy”*. That reads a bit harsh. Let’s replace this idea with *‘vulnerability’*. The language shifts from describing a person as being weak or inadequate to referring to them as being prone to damage. You are vulnerable when you’re in love. The brain interprets the slightest change in their behaviour as something being gravely wrong. Though, vulnerability isn’t necessarily bad. I believe it’s crucial in a relationship. We humans are not meant to go all our lives with sky high walls built around us. It is unsustainable and detrimental to our health. We need a safe space to be everything we’re not as a reasonable grown adult. This human-shaped safe space becomes our haven to be vulnerable and with that comes the need to be pathetic to protect it. Begging your partner to not leave you is equally pathetic as keeping their selective actions hidden from your family & friends because you want to protect how they perceive your partner. All of it is some form of pathetic, it’s just where you draw the line.

The point isn’t to shame anyone for behaving a certain way towards their own partner. Love, like most other things, is extremely grey. It is full of hypocritical actions and visceral reactions. I am no exception to it either. However, it is the first time in my life that I realise that being in love with my man comes with the ability to be pathetic. I know I’m in love because I know when push comes to shove, I will be pathetic. 

Very lovingly so.
