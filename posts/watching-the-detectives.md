I saw one too many reels of Cilian Murphy and Lucy Liu on Youtube from that 2000s romcom and decided I want to watch this movie. I was seated to watch the chemistry between these two gorgeous people.

I don’t remember the last time a movie had me at the edge of my seat throughout its running time.

Watching the Detectives is such an odd fever dream-like movie.

Lucy Liu plays a manic pixie dream girl whose whole personality is—I don’t own a tv set because I entertain myself by playing extreme pranks on people around me. Including a potential love interest.

Cilian finds this trait exhilarating, till he doesn’t. Since the very beginning Liu’s character Violet has no sense of boundaries. The first time she asks Neil on a date, she pretends to be wasted the moment he walks in just to see if he takes advantage of her. This is followed by the most ridiculous prank wherein she gets two actors to pretend to be cops who crackdown at Neil’s video store and badger him to get Violet’s number and when Neil, genuinely terrified, refuses, one of the actors pretends to unbuckle his pants and chases Neil as if he’s going to sexually assault him.

Neil (and I) are equally horrified to discover that this was all a prank. At this point I was about 40 minutes into the movie with no idea HOW this was a romantic comedy.

And just fyi, this isn’t the worst prank she plays.

## The whole movie is a hidden redemption arc ##

Neil Lewis owns a video-store and lives vicariously through the movies he watches. He is also perpetually surrounded by movie buffs who always hang out at his nearly run-down store. He seems to have been marinating in this echo chamber that reaffirms his belief that a life partner should be adventurous & free-spirited. While breaking up with his former girlfriend he references Butch Cassidy & the Sundance Kid, talking about how Katherine Ross joined Paul Newman and fleeing the country and settling anew. This is the only time we get a peek into what love looks like to Neil and how warped his perception is by the media.

Post that breakup, the film conveniently turns into a lowkey redemption arc as Neil gets bombarded with increasingly absurd pranks from Violet. What he clocks in as adventurous at first, quickly turns dangerous as Neil himself feels tense when he’s introducing Violet to his best friends. He cannot trust her to behave and he cannot keep up with her free-spirit.

It’s only when he runs into his ex-girlfriend that he realises that he had always wanted someone like Violet but turns out he doesn’t have the balls to keep up with a woman like that.

In the end, Neil was a normal guy yearning for a fictional version of a lover. He forgets that in movies you only have to bear with the antics of a free-spirited person for 2 hours. But in real life, you gotta face the consequences their actions have on everyone else. All the time.

## So much potential to be a thriller ##

With some editing and a change in the background score, this movie could totally qualify for a thriller. In fact, the more I think of it, the more I realise how much this movie could make for a really good unsettling creepy psychological horror.

Here are some scenes to prove my point:

- Violet always knows where to find Neil but does not give him her number till their 4th encounter
- The first time he follows her home, she has sneaked into an old person’s home and is feeding their cats. In a scene some time later, Neil points out that he hasn’t seen the lights on in that old person’s house for a few days now and wonders if they are okay
- Violet has a fish tank in the shape of a tv set and says she doesn’t need a television set to entertain her
- She casually trots in a cemetery using tombstones as stepping stones
- Her background and history is given no exposure whatsoever. She is mysterious and unpredictable and that is the depth of her character.

The movie most definitely gaslit me into thinking I’m crazy for making such a big deal out of Violet’s insane pranks.

I would’ve enjoyed this so much more if Violet was actually crazy OR if she was a normal girl but her adventures & pranks were all a figment of Neil’s imagination. Either way, I can name a dozen romcoms better than this.
